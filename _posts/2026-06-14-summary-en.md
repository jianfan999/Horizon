The U.S. Census Bureau has banned the use of noise infusion, a key differential privacy technique, in its published statistical products, reversing a policy adopted for the 2020 Census. This decision prioritizes data accuracy at the potential cost of individual privacy, sparking debate about the trade-off between usable statistics and protection against re-identification in an era of advanced data linkage. Noise infusion, or additive noise, is a method that adds carefully calibrated random noise to statistical outputs to ensure differential privacy. The ban applies to all statistical products published by the Census Bureau, not just the decennial census.

**Background**: Differential privacy (DP) is a mathematical framework that allows agencies to release aggregate statistics while limiting what can be inferred about any individual. It typically involves adding controlled noise to data. The Census Bureau had adopted DP for the 2020 Census, but concerns over data accuracy led to this policy reversal.
