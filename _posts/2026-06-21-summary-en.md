## [3D fiber micro-tweezer achieves force over 100,000x traditional optical tweezers](https://36kr.com/newsflashes/3861209206740228?f=rss) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers from Anhui University and USTC developed a femtosecond laser composite manufacturing method to build a 3D fiber micro-tweezer on commercial fiber ends, achieving force output over 100,000 times that of traditional optical tweezers. The research was published in Nature. This breakthrough enables high-precision, low-damage, and programmable 3D manipulation of microscale objects, vastly surpassing the force limitations of conventional optical tweezers. It could revolutionize microscale assembly, biomanipulation, and lab-on-fiber technologies. The 3D fiber micro-tweezer was fabricated directly on commercial optical fiber ends using femtosecond laser composite manufacturing. It achieves force output over 100,000 times greater than conventional optical tweezers, enabling precise manipulation and assembly of complex microstructures.

rss · 36氪 · Jun 20, 08:42

**Background**: Optical tweezers use a highly focused laser beam to trap and manipulate microscopic objects via momentum transfer from light. However, conventional optical tweezers have limited force output (typically piconewtons), restricting their use for larger or stiffer objects. Femtosecond laser composite manufacturing combines ultra-short pulse ablation with additional processing steps to create complex 3D microstructures on fiber tips.

This advancement could revolutionize the study of supermassive black holes and dark matter by making it possible to detect signals that were previously undetectable. It opens new avenues for fundamental physics and cosmology. The sensor uses atomic interferometry, where lasers act as beam splitters and mirrors for atoms, but the laser pulses themselves introduce noise. The new design effectively cancels this noise, allowing the atomic interference signal to be measured cleanly.

**Background**: An atom interferometer exploits the wave-like nature of atoms to measure forces and accelerations with high precision. In these devices, lasers are used to split and recombine atomic wave packets; the resulting interference pattern is sensitive to external influences. Traditional atom interferometers suffer from laser noise that can mask weak signals.
