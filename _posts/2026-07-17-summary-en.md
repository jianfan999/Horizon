## [JWST Detects First Atmosphere on Rocky Exoplanet LHS 1140b](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cy4kdd1e0ejo) ⭐️ 9.0/10

NASA's James Webb Space Telescope has confirmed the presence of an atmosphere on the rocky exoplanet LHS 1140b, located 48 light-years away in the habitable zone of its red dwarf star. This marks the first confirmed atmosphere on a rocky world in a habitable zone. This discovery is a major milestone in exoplanet science, as it demonstrates that rocky planets in habitable zones can retain atmospheres, a key requirement for potential habitability. It opens the door to studying the composition of such atmospheres and searching for biosignatures. LHS 1140b is a super-Earth about 5.6 times Earth's mass and orbits its star every 24.7 days. The atmosphere was detected using transmission spectroscopy during a transit, and the data rule out a mini-Neptune scenario, confirming a rocky composition with a thin atmosphere.

hackernews · neversaydie · Jul 17, 14:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48947560)

**Background**: Exoplanets are planets outside our solar system. The habitable zone is the region around a star where temperatures could allow liquid water on a planet's surface. LHS 1140b orbits a red dwarf star, which are cooler and smaller than the Sun, but often emit strong flares that can strip atmospheres. JWST's infrared capabilities allow it to analyze exoplanet atmospheres by studying starlight filtered through them.

## [Zilog Z80 Microprocessor Celebrates 50th Anniversary](https://goliath32.com/blog/z80.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Zilog Z80 microprocessor has reached its 50th anniversary, with a blog post and community discussions commemorating its impact on computing. The Z80 was a pivotal CPU powering iconic home computers like the TRS-80, ZX Spectrum, and MSX, and its instruction set continues to influence modern embedded systems and retrocomputing enthusiasts. The Z80 was binary compatible with Intel's 8080 but introduced a more extensive instruction set and register file, while some flag register behaviors differed. In April 2024, Zilog announced the discontinuation of standalone DIP Z80 chips.

hackernews · st_goliath · Jul 17, 19:41 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48951461)

**Background**: The Z80, introduced in 1976 by Zilog, became one of the most widely used 8-bit microprocessors in history. It powered countless home computers, game consoles, and industrial controllers. Its architecture inspired later CPUs and remains a popular choice for educational and hobbyist projects due to its simplicity and thorough documentation.
