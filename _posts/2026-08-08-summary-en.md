This is the first confirmed observation of a particle composed entirely of gluons, with no valence quarks. The discovery confirms a long-standing prediction of quantum chromodynamics and demonstrates that particles can be made purely of force. It marks a paradigm shift in understanding matter, with implications for fundamental physics and the Standard Model. Glueballs arise because gluons carry color charge and interact with each other via the strong force, unlike photons. They are notoriously difficult to distinguish from ordinary mesons in accelerator data, which is why it took 15 years to gather definitive evidence.

**Background**: Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is the theory of the strong force, which binds quarks inside protons and neutrons. Gluons are the force-carrying particles of QCD, and because they carry color charge, they can bind to each other to form glueballs. Physicists have expected these exotic particles for decades, but distinguishing them from other particles has been a major experimental challenge.

## [DNS Proposal Lets Domains Publicly Signal 'For Sale'](https://specification.website/spec/foundations/for-sale-dns/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A new IETF draft specification (draft-davids-forsalereg) proposes an operational convention where the reserved DNS leaf node name '_for-sale' is used to indicate that the parent domain name is available for purchase. The convention is designed to be easily deployed without affecting ongoing DNS operations. This convention gives domain owners a standardized, machine-readable way to advertise that a domain is for sale, which could streamline acquisitions and foster market transparency. However, it also raises concerns about UDRP arbitration, since publicly stating a domain is for sale may be used as evidence in trademark disputes and could encourage domain squatting. The '_for-sale' node is reserved, underscored, and globally scoped, and it can be deployed without affecting other DNS operations. Importantly, the document notes that absence of the record does not explicitly mean 'not for sale,' since most domains that are for sale do not currently have such a record.

hackernews · shaunpud · Aug 8, 13:26 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49221668)

**Background**: DNS (Domain Name System) is the internet's phonebook, mapping human-readable domain names to IP addresses through hierarchical name servers. The UDRP (Uniform Domain-Name Dispute-Resolution Policy) is an ICANN procedure that lets trademark holders challenge registrations they consider abusive or in bad faith, such as cybersquatting. The new draft proposes a lightweight DNS convention: if a domain owner creates a reserved '_for-sale' node, it signals that the parent domain is on the market. This may later be used as a factor in UDRP proceedings to determine whether a registration was made in bad faith.
