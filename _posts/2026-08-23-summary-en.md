## [How Complex Systems Fail: Seminal 1998 Essay on System Safety](https://how.complexsystems.fail/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The 1998 essay 'How Complex Systems Fail' by Richard I. Cook is being widely shared and discussed on Hacker News, reinforcing its status as a foundational text in resilience engineering and SRE. The essay's critique of root cause analysis and emphasis on human adaptation as a safety resource continue to shape how modern software and safety-critical industries approach reliability. It underpins practices such as chaos engineering and resilience engineering. The essay outlines 19 principles of complex system failure, including that systems run in a degraded mode most of the time and that 'failure-free operations require experience with failure.' The web version preserves the original text, including a typo ('THE') noted by a commenter.

hackernews · shortcrct · Aug 23, 15:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49409473)

**Background**: Complex systems are networks of interdependent components where failures are often emergent rather than traceable to a single root cause. The essay, written by patient-safety researcher Richard I. Cook, draws on decades of safety science, including Charles Perrow's 'Normal Accidents' theory, which argues that accidents are inevitable in tightly coupled, complex systems. These ideas influence resilience engineering, a field focused on how systems can adapt to surprise, and chaos engineering, which deliberately injects failures to build confidence in production systems.

AI-guided protein design allows researchers to build customized receptors on demand, potentially expanding the range of pathogens plants can detect.
