## [Tailcat: like netcat, but over Tailscale's data plane](https://github.com/tailscale/tailcat) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Tailscale has released Tailcat, a netcat-like tool and library that runs over Tailscale's data plane using WireGuard-encrypted point-to-point tunnels. One side acts as a listener and issues a short connection token; the other side uses that token to establish an end-to-end encrypted connection. Tailcat lowers the barrier to peer-to-peer encrypted data transfer between any two machines on a tailnet, without needing Tailscale's control plane. It is already proving practical and fun, as shown by a Minecraft mod that uses tailcat as its transport. Tailcat reuses Tailscale's open-source data-plane components such as magicsock, with DERP acting as a NAT-traversal side channel and relay of last resort. It is available both as a CLI tool and a Go library, and supplies a Nix environment for development.

hackernews · nderjung · Aug 26, 17:42 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49452990)

**Background**: Netcat is a classic Unix utility for reading and writing data across network connections, often used for debugging and simple file transfer. Tailscale builds a private network called a tailnet, where the control plane (Tailscale coordination service) manages devices and keys, while the data plane runs on each device and handles encrypted traffic. Tailcat reuses only the data-plane pieces—WireGuard encryption and NAT traversal—so it does not require logging in to a tailnet or coordinating through Tailscale's control plane.

## [Qwen3.8-Flash-Next previews Qwen4 architecture with 6B active parameters](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8-flash-next) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Qwen AI released Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, an experimental preview of the architecture that will underpin Qwen4. The model combines a 125B-parameter main model with 51B n-gram embeddings, activating only 6B parameters per token, and replaces previous attention mechanisms with Qwen Sparse Attention (QSA). This release demonstrates a promising direction for cost-efficient large language models, delivering competitive performance at very low inference cost—one user reported handling millions of cached input tokens for under a dollar. As an early look at Qwen4's architecture, it signals how the next generation of Qwen models may balance quality, memory, and compute. The model's total parameter count is roughly 176B (125B main plus 51B n-gram embeddings), but only 6B are activated per token. It uses micro-block-level Qwen Sparse Attention rather than token-level selection, and the community has raised questions about whether a 4-bit quantized version can fit in 128GB of unified memory.

hackernews · tosh · Aug 26, 12:52 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49448210)

**Background**: N-grams are contiguous sequences of n items—such as words or characters—from a text, and n-gram language models predict the next word based on a fixed-size window of previous words. N-gram embeddings extend this idea by vectorizing these substrings to capture linguistic and semantic information, which can improve model efficiency. Sparse attention, meanwhile, restricts each token to attend to a subset of other tokens, reducing compute compared to full attention. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next also continues Qwen's hybrid-model line, but swaps the previous Gated Attention for QSA.

## [Actinide becomes first startup to produce HALEU fuel](https://www.actinideinc.com/press/actinide-becomes-first-startup-to-ever-enrich-natural-uranium-to-produce-haleu) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Actinide Inc. announced it is the first startup to successfully enrich natural uranium into high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU). The milestone was achieved using a modernized calutron, an electromagnetic isotope separation technology. HALEU is the critical fuel required by most U.S. advanced nuclear reactors, with uranium-235 enrichment between 5% and 20%. A startup producing HALEU could help diversify the nuclear fuel supply chain, which is currently dependent on Russia for much of the world's HALEU. The company employs a calutron, a 1940s mass spectrometer design originally developed for the Manhattan Project, upgraded with modern control systems and electromagnets. Actinide's flagship commercial product is enriched ytterbium-176, a stable isotope used to produce lutetium-177 for targeted cancer therapies, which shows the company's expertise in isotope separation.

hackernews · dsalzman · Aug 26, 19:23 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49454419)

**Background**: HALEU is defined as uranium enriched to greater than 5% and less than 20% of the uranium-235 isotope. It is required by most advanced reactor designs in the United States to achieve smaller, more efficient reactor cores. Traditional uranium enrichment relies on gas centrifuges or gaseous diffusion, while calutrons use strong magnetic fields to separate isotopes by mass, a process that was historically energy-intensive but has been revitalized by modern control systems.
