## [Interactive LA Building-Date Map Reveals Urban Growth and Data Blind Spots](https://lax-skyline.parcelscope.net/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A new interactive visualization at lax-skyline.parcelscope.net maps the construction years of buildings in Los Angeles from 1880 to 2026, letting users watch the city grow one building at a time. The project gained wide attention in online discussion, scoring 197 points and drawing 95 comments about urban planning and data interpretation. The map transforms dry property records into an intuitive narrative about how land-use rules and transportation decisions shape a city. It has reopened public debate about Los Angeles's restrictive zoning, its dismantled rail network, and the roots of the region's housing affordability crisis. The visualization appears to rely on current Los Angeles County Assessor records, so it mostly reflects buildings that still stand; demolished or wholly replaced structures are absent, making some early neighborhoods look empty. Commenters also noted that Los Angeles once had more than 1,300 miles of rail transit, and that the area's major downzoning in the 1980s contributed to an artificial shortage of built space.

hackernews · rustywasm · Sep 7, 18:52 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49601655)

**Background**: Los Angeles's built landscape is the result of layered policy decisions: early-20th-century rail expansion, mid-century highway construction, and zoning rules that increasingly favored low-density single-family homes. Survivorship bias is a cognitive trap in which people draw conclusions only from what is visible and surviving; in construction-date data, an old neighborhood that was rebuilt building-by-building can appear to have no history at all. This is why watching a city "get built" from current property records alone can understate the activity of earlier eras.

## [AI titans' circular deals echo S&L 'daisy chains', warns opinion piece](https://www.scmp.com/opinion/world-opinion/article/3366149/ai-titans-circular-deals-are-starting-look-daisy-chains?utm_source=rss_feed) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An SCMP opinion piece warns that the AI industry's circular deals now resemble the 'daisy chains' that preceded the 1980s savings-and-loan crisis. It argues that interlocking multi-year commitments across financing, semiconductors, power, and data centers could produce similar systemic risks. If these circular deals inflate valuations while masking risk, a correction could ripple across AI supply chains, data-center construction, and tech financing, potentially endangering major tech companies and their investors. The warning draws attention to systemic fragility in an industry often viewed purely through a growth lens. The piece explicitly compares today's financial engineering—an 'incestuous ecosystem' of cross-company commitments—to the interconnected transactions of the 1980s. Historically, such arrangements helped inflate asset values before roughly one-third of US thrift banks failed.

rss · SCMP · Sep 7, 12:30

**Background**: During the 1980s savings-and-loan crisis, 'daisy chains' were circular or reciprocal transactions among thrift institutions and developers that inflated land values and concealed loan defaults, until the facade unraveled and around a third of US thrift banks failed. The AI industry now features major tech companies simultaneously investing in each other, signing chip purchase agreements, and leasing data-center capacity, often with the same partners. This creates a dense web of mutual dependency where a single major default or demand shock could propagate across many firms. The opinion piece argues this interlocking financial structure deserves regulatory and investor scrutiny before it becomes a systemic problem.

## [China Imposes Up to 99.2% Anti-Dumping Duties on Japanese Chipmaking Chemical](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3366661/china-hits-critical-japanese-chipmaking-chemical-steep-anti-dumping-measures?utm_source=rss_feed) ⭐️ 7.0/10

China's Ministry of Commerce announced provisional anti-dumping duties of up to 99.2% on dichlorosilane imported from Japan, effective Tuesday after an eight-month investigation. Importers must pay corresponding cash deposits on shipments from Japanese manufacturers including Denal Silane. This move escalates trade tensions in the semiconductor supply chain, targeting a niche but critical chemical used in chip manufacturing. It could raise costs for Chinese chipmakers and prompt Japan to respond, further fragmenting the global semiconductor materials market. The duties apply to dichlorosilane (DCS), a silicon precursor used in LPCVD processes to deposit silicon nitride films. Denal Silane is specifically named as a Japanese manufacturer affected; the preliminary ruling follows an eight-month anti-dumping investigation.

rss · SCMP · Sep 7, 11:27

**Background**: Dichlorosilane (DCS) is a chemical compound with formula H2SiCl2, commonly used in semiconductor manufacturing. It is often mixed with ammonia in low-pressure chemical vapor deposition (LPCVD) chambers to grow silicon nitride thin films. Anti-dumping duties are trade remedies allowed by WTO rules when exported goods are sold below fair value and harm domestic industries.

## [Caltech Mathathon: First Hackathon for Research-Level Math and Responsible AI](https://mathathonchallenge.com/index.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Caltech undergraduates are organizing the Caltech Mathathon, which they describe as the first hackathon ever devoted to research-level mathematics, with a declared focus on responsible AI use. The event invites teams to work on AI-assisted mathematical problems over an intensive 40-hour timeframe. This event connects LLM-driven mathematical research with the widely known hackathon format, potentially giving students a new path into AI-assisted discovery. Because it emphasizes responsible AI use, it could shape how emerging tools are taught and evaluated in academic settings. The organizers are Caltech undergraduates who do not represent Caltech, its departments, or their sponsors, and they receive no monetary compensation; all funding raised goes toward paying judges and participants. More details about the responsible-AI commitments are published on the event's FAQ page, while comments note the 40-hour waiting-on-LLM format is an unusual departure from classic hackathons.

hackernews · astroanax · Sep 7, 09:26 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49596055)

**Background**: A hackathon is typically a short, intensive event in which participants form teams to build software or solve problems. Research-level mathematics, by contrast, is usually slow, exploratory, and driven by deep human reasoning, often leading to the creation of proofs or the discovery of new structures. Large language models have recently shown promising performance on math problems, but using them to make original research contributions remains experimental. Caltech Mathathon sits at the intersection of these trends, asking participants to combine LLM assistance, mathematical insight, and careful ethical considerations in one competition.

**Discussion**: One organizer joined the discussion for an AMA and clarified that the team is independent, unpaid, and directs all funding to judges and participants. A recent Caltech graduate welcomed the attention, saying the CS department is weak in AI and that the event gives students a way to gain ML recognition outside classes. Others voiced skepticism—one commenter felt waiting for an LLM for 40 hours conflicts with the educational spirit of hackathons, while another applied as a team and views it as a good testbed for building mathematical harnesses that maximize model reasoning.

## [Huawei Launches Trifold Smartphone with Tau Scaling Law Kirin Chip](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3366650/huawei-unveils-new-trifold-smartphone-powered-tau-scaling-law-based-kirin-chip?utm_source=rss_feed) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Huawei unveiled a trifold smartphone powered by its new Kirin 9050 Pro processor at a Guangzhou launch event, with Richard Yu calling it the company's most powerful Kirin chip ever. This is the first smartphone to use a chip based on Huawei's proprietary Tau Scaling Law. The launch represents a major step in Huawei's drive for chip self-sufficiency, allowing the company to boost performance through its own scaling law despite restricted access to advanced foreign semiconductor equipment. The trifold form factor also positions Huawei to compete more aggressively with rivals like Samsung in the emerging foldable-device market. The Kirin 9050 Pro embodies Huawei's Tau Scaling Law, which reframes chip performance gains as a matter of time compression rather than simply transistor-density scaling, supported by technologies such as LogicFolding. Huawei says it achieves these gains through a multi-level co-optimization mechanism spanning semiconductor devices, circuits, chips, and systems.

rss · SCMP · Sep 7, 10:32

**Background**: Huawei has faced US sanctions that limit its access to advanced chipmaking tools, prompting the company to pursue alternative ways to improve silicon performance. The Tau Scaling Law is Huawei's proposed post-Moore's-law approach, asking how much time can be saved in electronic system operation rather than only how many transistors can be packed into a smaller space. A trifold smartphone can be used as a normal phone, a book-style foldable, or a tablet-like display when fully unfolded.
