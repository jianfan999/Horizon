## [Subvocal's smart glasses decode silently mouthed speech into text](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340088) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Subvocal Company, a YC F26 startup founded by two Georgia Tech CS students, Aarush and Arnav, demonstrated smart glasses that use radio-frequency sensing to decode silently mouthed — or even fully internal, lips-closed — speech into text at the speed of speech. The team started with an EMG face-electrode prototype built in 36 hours at Stanford's TreeHacks in February, then abandoned EMG for RF because of sweat, electrical interference, placement variability, and the obvious problem that nobody wants electrodes taped to their face. Silent speech input would remove the biggest practical barrier to voice-driven computing — the social awkwardness and privacy leak of talking out loud in libraries, offices, and public spaces — and could be transformative for people with speech impairments. If it works reliably across users in a normal glasses form factor, it turns dictation tools like Wispr Flow into something usable anywhere. The glasses emit radio waves similar to Bluetooth that pass through the face and are modulated by the tongue, lips, and soft palate during silent mouthing; the team had to build an extremely low-noise RF sensing stack and position antennas for maximal field overlap with articulators. The hardest remaining problem is cross-user generalization, since every person's facial anatomy produces very different RF signal patterns, which the founders say can only be solved by massively scaling data.

hackernews · aarushyadav · Nov 2, 17:16

**Background**: A silent speech interface is a system that lets people communicate without producing audible sound, typically by sensing articulatory muscle activity (EMG), neural signals, or — as here — radio-frequency perturbations inside the mouth. MIT Media Lab's AlterEgo was an influential earlier system that read speech-articulator muscles on the face, jaw, and neck via electrodes, and it inspired this team's first hackathon prototype. Practical silent speech systems have historically struggled with signal variability, sensor placement, and generalization across speakers, which is why most remain lab demos rather than products.

## [Ubuntu LTS Kernels Still Unpatched for Critical Linux 0day RCE](https://freenode.net/article/ubuntu-lts-kernels-still-unfixed-for-critical-cve-2026-98432) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Nearly three weeks after the disclosure of CVE-2026-98432, a critical remote-code-execution 0day in the Linux kernel, Ubuntu's LTS kernels remain unfixed and Canonical has published no patch timeline or official mitigation guidance. The gap has left administrators of long-term-support systems running a network-reachable RCE flaw with only unofficial workarounds. Ubuntu LTS is one of the most widely deployed server and cloud operating systems, so an unpatched, remotely exploitable kernel flaw leaves a very large install base exposed for an extended window. The episode also raises questions about disclosure transparency and patch responsiveness from a major distribution that many organizations rely on for stable, security-maintained infrastructure. Community members point to a raw-table PREROUTING filter using the iptables/nftables u32 match — `iptables -t raw -I PREROUTING -p tcp -m u32 ! --u32 "0>>24&0x0F=5" -j DROP` — as a way to drop packets before they reach the vulnerable kernel path, though such header-pattern rules are heuristic, TCP-only, and risk discarding legitimate traffic. Notably, Red Hat published an advisory (RHSB-2026) claiming its mitigation "reduces exposure to this vulnerability but cannot fully prevent it" without explaining the limitation, and Ubuntu has offered nothing comparable.

hackernews · majorchord · Nov 2, 18:39 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50341462)

**Background**: A CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures) identifier is the standard public label for a specific disclosed security flaw, and a "zero-day" describes a vulnerability that is being exploited or known before an official fix exists. Remote code execution (RCE) is among the most severe vulnerability classes, because an attacker who can run their own code over the network typically gains full control of the target host without any user interaction. Ubuntu LTS releases are supported for years and receive backported kernel patches rather than version upgrades, so a missing fix means every default install of that LTS series keeps the flaw until Canonical ships an updated kernel package.

## [Astra LLM agent reportedly reads and edits Qwen weights directly to boost math scores](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A blog post titled "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming" describes how Astra, an LLM agent, was given a small Qwen model's weights and tokenizer along with NumPy and an attention-inspection tool, and then edited the network's parameters directly rather than through gradient-based training. According to coverage of the work, adjusting roughly 0.016% of parameters lifted the model's MATH-500 score from 7.9% to 48.1% and removed repetitive reasoning loops. If it holds up, this suggests model improvement could shift from symbolic optimization algorithms like backpropagation toward an LLM reasoning over weight geometry directly, which would have major implications for model editing, interpretability and cheap post-training fixes. It also feeds the debate over whether frontier LLMs possess any native "understanding" of their own parameters. The approach reportedly requires no backpropagation or training loop, instead having the agent parse weight structure, identify a failure mode such as reasoning loops, and write corrections in a single reasoning pass on a very small (0.8B-parameter) model. The headline numbers come from a single benchmark (MATH-500) on an unusually tiny network, and critics argue the blog post and its promotion overstate how novel and how general the result is.

hackernews · dbreunig · Nov 2, 18:21 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50341174)

**Background**: Mainstream model improvement works by gradient descent: compute a loss on training data, backpropagate, and update millions of parameters over many steps. Weight-space learning instead tries to operate on the parameters themselves — editing, generating or selecting weights directly — and has existed in various forms for years. Qwen is Alibaba Cloud's family of open-weight language models, and the small 0.8B variant used here is cheap enough to experiment on; MATH-500 is a standard benchmark of 500 competition-style math problems used to measure reasoning ability.

## [Show HN: Browser-based voxel map lets you explore central Warsaw](https://p.migdal.pl/warsaw-vibe-voxel/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A developer published "Warsaw in voxels," an interactive browser-based 3D map that recreates central Warsaw as a walkable voxel city, built from OpenStreetMap, Poland's GUGiK/Geoportal LiDAR and aerial imagery, and Warsaw's official city tree database. Users can change the date, time of day and weather, and click on individual pedestrians to take control of them, with the full source released on GitHub as open source. The project shows how far open geospatial data plus WebGL can now go in turning a real city into a game-like, explorable world without any proprietary 3D assets or manual modeling. It is a compelling demonstration for urban planners, mapping enthusiasts and graphics developers, even though its current performance limits keep it a showcase rather than a broadly usable tool. The app is desktop-first (with some mobile support) and has a slow initial load, with commenters reporting sub-1 FPS in Firefox on Linux and roughly 120ms frame gaps on a mobile RTX 3080 Ti despite the simulation update itself running near 30fps. Building heights and trees come from LiDAR, colors from aerial imagery, and individual tree species from the city database drive seasonal foliage.

OpenStreetMap supplies vector data for buildings, streets, sidewalks and tram tracks, while LiDAR (light detection and ranging) scans from Poland's national Geoportal provide accurate building heights and tree canopies, with Polish LiDAR data at roughly 1 m resolution. Rendering such data in a browser typically relies on WebGL and techniques like instanced drawing to draw many cubes efficiently.

Anthropic has reportedly warned that it is "very close" to recursive self-improvement (RSI), the threshold at which an AI becomes good enough at training itself that AI capabilities begin to accelerate dramatically. The claim was reported by TechCrunch on November 2, 2026, in a brief item that did not include published benchmark data, a concrete timeline, or a technical definition of how the lab measures "very close." RSI is the threshold many researchers treat as the point where capability gains could become sudden rather than gradual, making oversight and evaluation far harder. If a frontier lab genuinely believes it is near that point, it strengthens the case for pre-deployment safety testing and could intensify competitive pressure on other labs to follow suit. The reporting is thin: there is no technical evidence, no published evaluation, and no clarification of whether "very close" means months or years, which matters because RSI is generally described as a spectrum rather than a single switch. Separately, the 2026 International AI Safety Report, drawing on more than 100 independent experts, lists loss of control through AI recursive self-improvement among the most consequential national-security-level risks of advanced AI.

rss · TechCrunch · Nov 2, 22:14

**Background**: Recursive self-improvement describes a process in which an AI system iteratively designs a more capable successor, which in turn designs an even more capable one. The theoretical endpoint is an "intelligence explosion," where each cycle of improvement is faster than the last and could plausibly produce an artificial superintelligence. Because that feedback loop could outpace human review, some commentators — often called AI doomers — regard RSI as the point of no return, while other researchers note that no true self-sustaining intelligence explosion has yet been demonstrated.
