## [FAA Issues Nationwide Ground Stop Over Telecom Outage](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/faa-orders-nationwide-ground-stop-departing-flights-reasons-safety-rcna609247) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The ground stop was lifted later the same day after the disruption was addressed. A nationwide ground stop is one of the most drastic operational actions the FAA can take, and because it froze departures across the entire National Airspace System, it cascaded into delays and cancellations for thousands of flights and stranded passengers at major hubs. It also raises hard questions about the resilience of the aging, contractor-operated telecommunications backbone that air traffic control depends on every day. Because controllers rely on these voice and data links for both radio communications and radar feeds, a network failure can quickly become a safety-of-flight issue rather than a mere scheduling inconvenience.

## [Hobbyist SDR rig demodulates and transcribes the entire VHF airband near Orcas Island](https://orcasairband.com/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A hobbyist developer (mmaunder) launched orcasairband.com, a live airband monitoring site that feeds an ETTUS B210 software-defined radio into a dual RTX 4090 machine, where CUDA polyphase channelizer code splits the entire VHF airband into channels, demodulates them, detects transmissions and discards idle data, and runs speech-to-text on the surviving audio. During the launch the system unexpectedly captured real chatter surrounding a ground stop affecting Victoria and Vancouver, and the author posted raw, unedited transcripts of the relevant transmissions. The project shows that a single consumer-grade GPU box can now monitor an entire 18 MHz aviation band continuously in real time, a task that previously required racks of dedicated receivers or expensive commercial wideband systems. That lowers the barrier for hobbyists, journalists and researchers who want an independent, publicly readable record of air-traffic communications during disruptions. The pipeline relies on GPU-accelerated polyphase channelization to cover the whole band at once rather than scanning, and it only keeps audio that passes a transmission-detection stage before transcription. Quality varies widely — the posted transcripts are explicitly raw and unedited, several fragments are marked unintelligible, and one commenter notes that roughly half the audio is hard to make out even for a human listener.

hackernews · mmaunder · Nov 13, 22:04 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50490593)

**Background**: VHF airband is the 118–136 MHz slice of spectrum where air traffic controllers and pilots talk; each airport tower, ground, approach and en-route center uses its own assigned channel, so covering a region means monitoring dozens of frequencies at once. Software-defined radio (SDR) replaces fixed analog circuitry with digitized RF processed in software, and a polyphase channelizer is a digital filter-bank technique that splits one wideband sample stream into many narrow channels efficiently — work that maps well onto GPUs. A ground stop is an air-traffic-control order halting departures (or arrivals) at an airport or region, typically due to weather, congestion or a system outage, as when the FAA halts flights nationwide.

## [Granzyme K drives tissue aging without triggering systemic inflammation](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03638-3) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A Nature news article reports on a study published in Immunity showing that Granzyme K (GzmK), a serine protease markedly upregulated in aged memory CD8+ T cells, drives age-related tissue remodeling and functional decline in mice. Notably, mice lacking GzmK still displayed persistent immune aging phenotypes yet had markedly attenuated organ aging, effectively uncoupling immune aging from tissue aging. The finding suggests that tissue decline during aging is not merely a downstream consequence of chronic low-grade inflammation (inflammaging), and that a single secreted enzyme could be a tractable target for slowing organ aging. If it holds up, it would reframe aging research around tissue-specific mediators rather than treating systemic inflammation as the master driver, with implications for longevity and age-related disease therapeutics. The work is an early mouse study, and the paper's abstract stresses nuance: GzmK deficiency attenuated organ aging while immune aging and systemic inflammaging persisted, identifying GzmK as a tissue-specific driver of organismal aging rather than of immune aging. The underlying paper sits behind a paywall, and Granzyme K remains a relatively poorly characterized "orphan granzyme," so its molecular functions are still being mapped.

hackernews · gumby · Nov 13, 19:25 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50488470)

**Background**: Granzymes are serine proteases that cytotoxic T cells and natural killer cells release from their granules, classically to induce apoptosis in target cells; Granzyme K is one of the less-studied members of this family, sometimes called an orphan granzyme until recent interest in its roles in aging, rheumatoid arthritis, and airway inflammation. Aging research has long focused on "inflammaging," the chronic low-grade inflammation that accompanies old age and is thought to drive tissue decline. This study asks whether a molecule made by aged immune cells can damage tissues directly, independently of the inflammation itself.

## [1960 "Doomsday" Population Paper Resurfaces as a Forecasting Cautionary Tale](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.132.3436.1291) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A 1960 Science paper by Heinz von Foerster, Patricia M. Mora and Lawrence W. Amiot, provocatively titled "Doomsday: Friday, 13 November, A.D. 2026," has resurfaced on Hacker News (459 points, 313 comments) as its predicted date approaches. The paper extrapolated then-available world population data to a finite-time singularity at which human population would become literally infinite on that exact Friday. The paper has become a textbook cautionary tale about naive curve fitting: global fertility has fallen sharply since the 1960s, and many industrialized countries now worry about population decline rather than explosion. Its revival is a timely reminder that extrapolating past trends — a habit still common in forecasts about AI, data centers and resource use — can produce confident but wildly wrong dates. Von Foerster's team found their hyperbolic equation fit roughly 25 data points from the birth of Jesus to 1958, leaving only about 7% of the variance unexplained, and follow-up letters in Science in 1961 and 1962 claimed the curve was still on track, with the data continuing to fit until about 1973. The model has the form N(t) = C/(t_s − t), which diverges to infinity as t approaches the finite singularity t_s — a mathematical property that makes the prediction essentially unfalsifiable in its final years.

hackernews · voxleone · Nov 13, 12:10 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50482379)

**Background**: Heinz von Foerster was a physicist and cyberneticist at the University of Illinois, best known for founding the Biological Computer Laboratory and for work on self-organizing systems. Hyperbolic growth describes a quantity whose growth rate accelerates so fast that it reaches a finite-time singularity, i.e. it diverges to infinity at a specific moment rather than growing exponentially forever. The 1960s and early 1970s were dominated by fears of overpopulation, an anxiety reflected in works such as Paul Ehrlich's "The Population Bomb" and the film "Soylent Green."

## [Practitioner runs 600 experiments probing JEPA world models for planning](https://haddyalchaer.com/jepa) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A practitioner published a detailed write-up documenting roughly 600 hands-on experiments testing whether JEPA world models can be used for planning and control, including a deliberately harder setup where each task starts from one demonstration and takes its goal from another. The post also describes an interactive demo where a block could be placed anywhere and any goal selected, and it drew a 217-point Hacker News discussion with 36 comments. Most JEPA coverage is theoretical or benchmark-driven, so a large batch of practitioner-level experiments gives engineers a concrete sense of where latent world models help and where they break down for planning. The discussion it triggered is also useful because it reframes JEPA in terms familiar to control engineers, which could shape how the robotics and RL communities evaluate such architectures. The main methodological caveat raised is that the harder test still draws its goals from frames of expert demonstrations rather than requiring movement between two arbitrary states, which limits how much the results say about general goal-conditioned planning. Commenters also noted that the phenomena described — hierarchical planning, sub-goals and action chunking — are long-established ideas rather than new discoveries, and several readers complained that the site's color theme was hard to read.

hackernews · halcdev · Nov 13, 17:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50487185)

**Background**: JEPA, or Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture, is a self-supervised learning framework introduced by Yann LeCun and Meta AI that predicts abstract representations of inputs in a latent space instead of reconstructing raw pixels or generating tokens, with the explicit goal of building world models that capture what matters for understanding, planning and reasoning. Model predictive control (MPC) is a classical control technique that optimizes a cost function over a finite time horizon using a dynamic model of the system, applies only the first action, and then re-plans — a loop commenters found structurally very similar to JEPA-style planning with a learned model.
