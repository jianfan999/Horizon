## [Chinese scientists fired after girl, 6, dies in first in-brain gene-editing attempt](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/science/article/3374429/chinese-scientists-fired-after-child-dies-worlds-first-brain-gene-editing-attempt) ⭐️ 9.0/10

An official investigation ordered by the Shanghai municipal party committee and local government concluded on Friday that researchers behind the world's first attempt to edit genes inside a child's brain — a six-year-old girl in Shanghai who died last year — had broken research rules and taken money from her family. The scientists were fired after investigators found they had failed to disclose worrying results from monkey safety tests to the girl's own doctors before the procedure. This is the first known fatality linked to an in-brain gene-editing procedure, and the official finding of concealed preclinical safety data strikes at the credibility of Chinese clinical research oversight at a time when China is a major hub for gene-editing trials. It is likely to intensify scrutiny of ethical review, informed consent, and patient-paid experimental treatments, with ripple effects on how gene-editing therapies are regulated and perceived globally. The investigation specifically faulted the team for withholding adverse monkey-test findings from the treating clinicians, and noted that the family had paid for the experimental procedure — a red flag under rules governing experimental therapies. The case involved direct, in-brain delivery of gene-editing agents, a far riskier route than the ex vivo editing used in better-known trials, where immune reactions and dose-related toxicity are major known hazards.

rss · SCMP · Nov 14, 06:23

**Background**: In vivo gene editing means modifying genes directly inside a patient's body, typically by packaging editing tools into adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors or lipid nanoparticles; AAV-based delivery is the most common approach for reaching the brain, but it carries risks of dose-dependent toxicity and immune responses, especially at high doses. Because such treatments are still experimental, they must go through ethics committee review and China's NMPA clinical trial authorization process, and preclinical animal safety data are expected to inform both regulators and treating physicians. The 2018 He Jiankui case, in which babies were gene-edited as embryos, already made research ethics in China a globally watched issue.

## [SpaceX catches Starship upper stage to end Flight 15](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15) ⭐️ 8.0/10

At the end of Starship Flight 15, SpaceX's launch-tower arms caught the Starship upper stage (Ship 42) after roughly 22 hours in space, the first time a Starship returning from space was captured in mid-air. The Super Heavy booster's catch was called off, and the booster instead ended its flight in a simulated landing over the Gulf of Mexico that culminated in an explosion. Successfully recovering the upper stage is essential to SpaceX's goal of reusing the entire launch system, not just the booster, and could eventually lower the cost of lifting payloads to orbit. However, the missed booster catch shows that the planned “double catch” and rapid full-stack reuse remain unproven. Flight 15 used Ship 42 and Booster 22, both Block 3 vehicles; the ship completed about 14 orbits at roughly 275 km before reentry and the tower catch. The booster catch was skipped, so the key open questions are heat-shield durability after reentry and how quickly the caught ship can be inspected, refurbished and flown again.

hackernews · ls612 · Nov 14, 21:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50500963)

**Background**: Starship is SpaceX's two-stage, fully reusable launch system: the Super Heavy booster lifts the Starship upper stage, which can carry payloads and people. SpaceX's “Mechazilla” launch tower uses giant chopstick-like arms to catch returning stages, avoiding heavy landing legs and aiming for fast turnaround. Earlier tests progressed from ocean splashdowns toward tower catches; Flight 15 was intended to move from booster recovery to recovering the ship as well. By contrast, NASA's Space Shuttle was only partially reusable, so full and rapid reuse is the central economic bet behind Starship.

## [Lovable-built apps leak passports and 200K private messages via weak RLS](https://www.crissyfield.de/research/lovable-en/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Security researchers at Crissy Field found that web apps built with the AI app builder Lovable had insufficient Row-Level Security, exposing sensitive data including passport details and more than 200,000 private messages. The exposures were reachable simply by inspecting network calls in the browser, requiring no sophisticated hacking skill. This is one of the clearest real-world demonstrations that AI-generated apps can ship with insecure defaults at scale, and because there are huge numbers of such apps being created daily, exposure is both widespread and easy to exploit. It puts pressure on AI app builders and backend platforms like Supabase to adopt deny-by-default configurations rather than relying on end users to configure security correctly. The root cause was insufficient Row-Level Security in the generated apps, which Lovable says its automatic pre-publish scanning and optional deeper SAST scan are designed to catch, with users able to click "Fix" or enable auto-fix and block publishing. Commenters noted that Supabase's permissive defaults for existing projects may still apply despite a claimed fix on October 30, so older projects remain at risk.

hackernews · armcat · Nov 14, 17:43 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50499017)

**Background**: Lovable is an AI app builder that turns a plain-language prompt into a working full-stack app, complete with interface, database and logic, typically backed by Supabase. Supabase is an open-source Firebase alternative that provides a hosted PostgreSQL database, authentication, storage and APIs. Row-Level Security is a PostgreSQL feature that restricts which rows a given user or role can read or write; if policies are missing or too permissive, the database API will happily return other users' data to anyone who asks.

## [Google Project Zero to cut vulnerability disclosure window to 30 days by 2028](https://projectzero.google/2026/11/disclosure-policy.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google Project Zero announced a graduated tightening of its disclosure policy: the current "Full 90" rule stays in effect today, shifting to "Full 60" on February 2, 2027, and finally to "Full 30" on January 3, 2028, meaning vulnerabilities will be published 30 days after vendors are notified. This is a staged acceleration rather than a one-off change, giving the industry roughly two years to adapt. Project Zero is the team that effectively established the 90-day disclosure deadline as an industry norm, so compressing it to 30 days puts direct pressure on vendors, Linux distributions and open-source maintainers to patch and ship fixes far faster. A 30-day window could ripple through downstream release cycles, enterprise patch-management schedules and coordinated disclosure practices across the whole software ecosystem. The policy governs when Project Zero publishes details of bugs it has already reported privately to the vendor, so the change mainly affects the post-report grace period rather than the discovery process itself. The schedule is fixed by explicit dates (February 2, 2027 and January 3, 2028), which leaves slower-moving downstreams such as Linux distributions with a defined deadline to restructure their patch pipelines.

hackernews · hacb · Nov 14, 11:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50496051)

**Background**: Project Zero is a Google security research team announced in July 2014 whose analysts hunt for zero-day vulnerabilities in widely used software, including products made by other companies. In 2015 the team adopted a 90-day disclosure deadline — publish details 90 days after privately notifying the vendor — and later added a short grace period for fixes that were close to ready; that deadline became a de facto standard many vendors and researchers now plan around. The practice sits inside the broader norm of coordinated vulnerability disclosure (CVD), in which a flaw is reported privately and given time to be patched before details become public. Debate over how long that buffer should be has persisted for a decade, usually framed as a trade-off between giving vendors time and limiting how long users remain exposed.

## [Show HN: DeepSeek V3 Inference Reimplemented in ~200 Lines of Pure JAX](https://github.com/Vaibhaav-Tiwari/deepseek-v3-jax) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Community members report that it runs end-to-end on AMD GPUs through ROCm, which the author and commenters treat primarily as a first-principles learning exercise. DeepSeek V3 is a 671B-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model whose official codebase is large and complex, so a minimal readable implementation lowers the barrier for engineers who want to understand how such a model actually computes a token. It also serves as a concrete data point that JAX plus ROCm can work as a non-CUDA path for local deep learning on consumer AMD cards. This is an educational, minimal implementation rather than a performance-oriented serving stack, so throughput and memory efficiency are not its goals. For AMD hardware, commenters note it required specific environment tweaks — for example an RX 7600 XT 16GB needed HSA_OVERRIDE_GFX_VERSION=11.0.0 so JAX's ROCm plugin would recognize the GPU.

**Background**: DeepSeek V3 is a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language model with 671B total parameters, of which only about 37B are activated for each token, which is what makes inference comparatively cheap relative to model size. JAX is an open-source numerical computing library that extends NumPy with automatic differentiation and XLA compilation, making it popular for high-performance ML research. ROCm is AMD's open-source GPU compute stack, positioned as an alternative to NVIDIA's CUDA, and JAX support on it has historically been less mature than on CUDA.

Offensive cyber capability in a model usually means the ability to write exploits, probe for vulnerabilities, or automate attack steps, whereas defensive use covers tasks like threat analysis, log review, and patch guidance.

## [Blog maps software job titles into an evolving family tree](https://zwischenzugs.com/2026/11/03/a-family-tree-of-software-job-titles/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A blog post on zwischenzugs.com published on November 3, 2026 lays out software industry job titles as a family tree, showing how roles such as DevOps Engineer, Scrum Master, Site Reliability Engineer and Software Engineer branch off from and merge into one another over time. The piece sparked a Hacker News discussion (scoring 6/10) about title inflation, the meaninglessness of many labels, and how the same title means different things at different companies. Job titles are the primary signal used in hiring, salary benchmarking and internal leveling, so widespread disagreement about what they mean makes it harder for candidates to judge roles and for companies to compare talent. The discussion reflects a broader trend in which titles multiply and inflate while the underlying skills — debugging, systems thinking, handling ambiguity — stay relatively stable. Commenters noted that Scrum Master and Product Owner certifications now feel as dated as the 1990s "webmaster" title, and that DevOps has fragmented so much that its meaning varies from org to org and even between teams inside the same company. One commenter argued the tree should be read as a constantly evolving graph, since roles like SRE were barely a niche when their own career began.

"Job title inflation" describes the practice of granting grander titles without matching pay or responsibility, which can leave employees unable to move to equivalent roles elsewhere. The debate over what a title actually certifies — process knowledge versus hands-on engineering ability — is at the center of the discussion.

The film covers KDE's origins as the "Kool Desktop Environment" volunteer call on Usenet, through the difficult KDE 4 transition, to today's Plasma era. The documentary gives a cultural record of one of the longest-running free software desktop projects, showing how a volunteer community sustained a large C++ codebase and a Qt-based stack for decades. Its discussion also offers a case study in open-source governance and long-term maintainer motivation that extends well beyond KDE itself.
