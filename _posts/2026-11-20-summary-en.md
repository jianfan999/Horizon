## [Anthropic says Claude derived results on two open black hole physics problems](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Anthropic published a research post reporting that Claude derived results bearing on two open problems in black hole physics: a bootstrap-style calculation, and an upper bound on black hole mass consistent with a given central charge. The claim was notable enough that it appeared as a bullet point in the Fable 5.2 announcement. If frontier models can contribute genuinely novel theoretical physics results, that is a meaningful signal for AI-for-science workflows and for how AI labs market model capability. It also raises hard questions about attribution and reproducibility, since the announcement frames the work as the model solving problems rather than as a human-AI research collaboration. Anthropic itself states that neither result describes our own universe directly and neither has practical applications, though both feed a long-running theoretical effort to reconcile quantum mechanics with relativity. Commenters noted that the word "prompt" does not appear in the blog post, either abstract, or either paper, and that the bootstrap paper in particular is very hard to read.

hackernews · cybermango · Nov 19, 17:30 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50563148)

**Background**: Bootstrap programs try to pin down physical theories from consistency conditions — symmetries, unitarity, crossing symmetry — rather than by brute-force calculation, and they have been a major tool in conformal field theory and quantum gravity research. The central charge is a parameter characterizing a conformal field theory, roughly counting its degrees of freedom, and via holographic dualities it maps onto properties of the corresponding gravitational theory. An upper bound on black hole mass for a given central charge matters because theories predicting masses above it cannot correspond to a consistent theory of quantum gravity.

**Discussion**: Sentiment on Hacker News is skeptical of the framing rather than of the physics: commenters object to the anthropomorphizing "Claude solves" headline, contrasting it with "it broke out of the sandbox" versus "we failed to build an adequate sandbox", and point out that no prompt details were disclosed. One reader who actually read the papers found the bootstrap result to be an impressive but error-prone calculation and the black hole mass bound considerably more interesting, while others lamented that most of the ~190 comments were meta-discussion rather than engagement with the results.

Few technical specifics were disclosed, and commenters were unable to pin down which specific clinical trial or virus construct the report refers to. Pancreatic cancer is one of the most lethal solid tumors, with most patients diagnosed too late for surgery and five-year survival in the single digits, so any signal of disease stabilization draws intense interest. **Background**: Oncolytic viruses are viruses that preferentially infect and kill cancer cells while largely sparing normal tissue; they can occur naturally or be engineered in the lab, and they work both by directly lysing tumor cells and by stimulating an immune response against the tumor. In pancreatic cancer, most published trials have been early-phase studies testing safety and dose escalation, including intraoperative direct intratumoral injection of viruses such as the HF10 adenovirus in patients with unresectable disease. Pancreatic tumors are notoriously hard to treat because they are usually detected late, are surrounded by dense fibrous stroma that blocks drug and virus penetration, and suppress local immune responses.

## [Synology Discloses Critical OAuth API Flaw in DSM, Urges Users to Disable OAuth Service](https://www.synology.com/en-uk/security/advisory/Synology_SA_26_21) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Synology published security advisory SA_26_21 disclosing CVE-2026-120382, an improper neutralization of argument delimiters in a command (CWE-88) vulnerability in the OAuth API of DiskStation Manager, rated CVSS 9.8 and exploitable remotely without authentication. The flaw affects DSM builds before 6.2.4-25556-9, 7.1.1-42962-10, 7.2.1-69057-14, 7.2.2-72806-11, 7.3.2-86009-6 and 7.4.1-90080-2, and Synology's interim mitigation is to uninstall or stop the OAuth Service package. Because the OAuth Service package ships installed by default on many DSM systems, a large number of NAS devices may be exposed even if owners never consciously configured OAuth, and successful exploitation allows attackers to read or write arbitrary files or mount denial-of-service attacks. The advisory lands amid a broader wave of ransomware and botnet campaigns targeting internet-facing NAS appliances, so patching or disabling the service is urgent for anyone running Synology hardware. The vulnerability is a command argument-delimiter injection issue in the OAuth API, meaning user-supplied input could be leveraged to construct or alter shell commands executed by the NAS. Synology lists specific fixed DSM builds for each branch, and for older units whose fix status remains "Ongoing" the only guidance is to remove the OAuth Service package via Package Center, accepting the loss of any OAuth-dependent integrations.

hackernews · choult · Nov 19, 22:13 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50567209)

**Background**: Synology DiskStation Manager (DSM) is the Linux-based operating system running on Synology NAS appliances, and packages such as OAuth Service extend it with extra capabilities. The OAuth Service handles authentication flows that connect the NAS to third-party applications, which is why its API accepts external input. Command injection (CWE-78/CWE-88) is one of the oldest and most common vulnerability classes: when an application builds a shell command from untrusted input without properly escaping delimiters, an attacker can append or alter arguments and run their own commands with the service's privileges.

## [UK tribunal forces government to admit secret Apple backdoor order](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/u-k-government-forced-to-disclose-secret-apple-backdoor-order/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On November 19, 2026, the UK's Investigatory Powers Tribunal (IPT) ruled that the Home Office must confirm it served Apple with a secret Technical Capability Notice (TCN) demanding access to encrypted user data, rejecting the government's argument that disclosure would damage national security. The decision forces ministers to publicly admit for the first time that such an order exists, after Apple launched a fresh legal challenge in July over a second TCN. The ruling strikes at the core secrecy of the UK's surveillance machinery and shifts the encryption-backdoor debate from rumour into a documented public record, setting a precedent that could make future TCNs harder to keep hidden. Because a TCN aimed at Apple could in principle affect iCloud data for users worldwide, the case is being watched closely by privacy advocates, other governments, and technology firms weighing whether to exit the UK market. The tribunal found the government failed to demonstrate that confirming the demand would harm national security, though the ruling concerns the existence of the order rather than the substantive legality of the TCN itself, which remains contested in separate proceedings. TCNs are issued under section 253 of the Investigatory Powers Act 2016 and can legally require a provider to remove encryption it applies, subject to necessity, proportionality, feasibility and cost tests plus approval by a Judicial Commissioner.

rss · TechCrunch · Nov 19, 15:32

**Background**: A Technical Capability Notice is a UK instrument that compels a communications provider to build or maintain the technical ability to comply with future interception warrants — in practice, the power to hand over readable, decrypted data. The Investigatory Powers Act 2016 underpins this regime, and the accompanying notices code of practice explicitly contemplates obligations relating to encryption. Reports indicate the Home Office first issued a TCN to Apple, dropped it amid a diplomatic dispute with Washington, then issued a second notice that Apple challenged in July 2026, arguing the government should not be able to refuse even to confirm whether such an order exists.

This study pushes the concern one step further, examining not just transcription but the scores AI models assign when asked to evaluate speech — a task increasingly delegated to large language models acting as graders. Accent bias research has largely focused on whether systems understand accented speech; this work suggests bias can persist in judgment even when comprehension is flawless.

ICJ President Yuji Iwasawa presented the legal basis for the decision, grounding it in the 1951 Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide. This is the first time the world's top court has formally found a state responsible for genocide, making it a landmark precedent in international law and human-rights accountability. The ruling raises pressure on Myanmar's military government and strengthens the legal footing for future prosecutions and for states invoking obligations under the Genocide Convention. The decision is framed as an interpretation of the 1951 Genocide Convention, so its practical force depends on how subsequent proceedings and state compliance unfold rather than on immediate enforcement by the court. The death toll cited — at least 10,000 Rohingya killed in 2017 — underpins the finding, which came after years of litigation over the crackdown in Rakhine State.

rss · SCMP · Nov 19, 11:52
